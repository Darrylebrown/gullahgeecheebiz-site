Sweetgrass Basket Weaving: The Living Art of Gullah Craft

Published August 30, 2026 · Gullah Geechee Biz

Sweetgrass baskets are among the most recognizable symbols of Gullah Geechee cultural heritage, and for good reason. The coiled basket tradition — using native sweetgrass, bullrush, and pine needles — connects directly to West African basket-making techniques that enslaved people brought to the Lowcountry. Historians trace this craft back to regions in present-day Senegal, Gambia, and Guinea-Bissau, where similar coiling methods were used for rice winnowing, storage, and ceremonial purposes.

The materials themselves carry cultural significance. Sweetgrass (_Hierochloe odorata_) was harvested from coastal marshes and valued not only for its fragrance but for its symbolic role as a purifying herb. Bullrush and pine needles provided structural integrity, while palmetto fronds and other grasses offered additional decorative possibilities. The gathering of these materials was traditionally a communal activity, reinforcing family and community bonds through shared labor.

Basket forms followed function: the rice basket for winnowing grain, the feather basket for storing clothing, the egg basket for collecting poultry produce, and the Market Basket — the iconic spiral-topped form — which emerged during the antebellum period when enslaved people sold their woven goods in Charleston markets. Each form carries specific technical requirements, and mastery of the coil stitch — wrapping coiled fiber with additional thread in a continuous spiral — requires years of practice.

Today, Gullah Geechee basket makers continue this tradition while adapting to contemporary markets and cultural preservation goals. Organizations like the Penn Center and the Gibbes Museum support artisan training programs, while federal recognition through the Gullah Geechee Cultural Heritage Corridor Act affirms the national significance of this craft. When you encounter a sweetgrass basket, you are holding a tangible link to centuries of artistic innovation and cultural resilience.
