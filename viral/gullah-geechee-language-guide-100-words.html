Gullah Geechee Language Guide: 100 Essential Words and Phrases

Published August 31, 2026 · Gullah Geechee Biz

Gullah is one of the most distinctive African American languages in the world. Born from West African languages including Mende, Temne, Igbo, and Wolof, it preserves linguistic features lost in standard American English. Here are 100 essential words to help you connect with this living heritage.

Greetings and Daily Expressions

  • Yoh wah do? — How are you?
  • Mornin' — Good morning (used throughout the day)
  • Evenin' — Good evening
  • Bless yo' heart — Expression of affection or sympathy
  • How yah do? — How are you doing?
  • I's good — I'm good
  • Walk good — Goodbye (lit. "walk carefully")
  • Talk later — See you later

Family Terms

  • Bunnu — Body
  • Man — Husband / male partner
  • Woman — Wife / female partner
  • Children — Kids / offspring
  • Baby — Infant
  • Granny — Grandmother
  • Granddaddy — Grandfather
  • Auntie — Mother's sister or close family friend
  • Uncle — Father's brother or close family friend
  • Cousin — Child of aunt/uncle

Food Words

  • Red rice — Tomato-based rice dish with roots in West Africa
  • Hoppin' John — Black-eyed peas and rice
  • Fried chicken — Sunday dinner staple
  • Collard greens — Slow-cooked leafy greens
  • Corn bread — Cast-iron skillet bread
  • Gumbo — Okra-thickened stew
  • Shrimp and grits — Lowcountry breakfast
  • Pimento cheese — Spread for sandwiches
  • Sweet tea — Iced tea with sugar
  • Coffee milk — Coffee with cream

Spiritual and Cultural Terms

  • Ring shout — Circular worship dance predating jazz
  • Devil — Evil spirit / bad person
  • Blessed — Happy / fortunate
  • Thank Yah — Thank you
  • Glory be — Expression of praise
  • Lord have mercy — Expression of sympathy
