Beyond the Resorts: Authentic Gullah Geechee Tourism Guide

Published August 09, 2026 · Gullah Geechee Biz

Hilton Head, Charleston, and Savannah welcome millions of visitors every year. Most come for the golf, the beaches, and the resorts — and most leave without ever meeting the culture that shaped this land long before the first hotel went up.

We are the Gullah Geechee people — descendants of West and Central Africans who built the rice, indigo, and Sea Island cotton economies of the Lowcountry. We are not a relic of the past. We are a living culture with our own language, our own food, our own music, and a homeland that stretches along the coast from Pender County, North Carolina, to St. Johns County, Florida — a region Congress formally recognized in 2006 as the Gullah Geechee Cultural Heritage Corridor.

That corridor is where authentic travel happens. On St. Helena Island, the Penn Center has carried our story for more than a century, from its founding as one of the nation's first schools for freed people to its role today as a cultural and educational heart of the Sea Islands. Along Highway 17, roadside stands still hold sweetgrass baskets coiled by the same hands — generation after generation — that have woven this art for over three hundred years.

Real cultural tourism means going to the praise houses, tasting red rice and okra soup, hearing the Gullah language spoken, and buying directly from the weavers, the cooks, and the storytellers who keep these traditions alive. It means choosing experiences that put money back into our communities rather than into absentee owners.

There's a growing movement toward exactly this — travelers who want to meet the people behind the Lowcountry instead of just photographing it. We welcome that movement, and we point it in the right direction.

Our Travel Magazine covers the best Gullah Geechee experiences, from sweetgrass basket demonstrations on St. Helena to Gullah cuisine tours in Charleston. Our books are the definitive guides to our culture, written from the inside.
