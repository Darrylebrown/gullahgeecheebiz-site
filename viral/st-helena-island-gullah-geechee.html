St. Helena Island: The Cultural Capital of Gullah Geechee

Updated August 2026 · Gullah Geechee Biz · Travel & Heritage

If you want to experience the real Gullah Geechee culture, St. Helena Island, South Carolina is the destination. This sea island in Beaufort County has been a center of African American heritage since before the Civil War — home to Penn Center, celebrated sweetgrass basket weavers, and the living heart of the Gullah language and traditions.

1. Penn Center — A National Historic Landmark

Founded in 1862 as one of the first schools for formerly enslaved African Americans, Penn Center became a refuge where Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. and the SCLC held retreats during the Civil Rights Movement. Its campus, archives, and museum on St. Helena Island tell the story of freedom, education, and Black community leadership like nowhere else.

2. Sweetgrass Basket Stands

Along the roads of St. Helena and nearby Mount Pleasant, Gullah weavers sell coiled sweetgrass baskets — a craft passed down from West Africa for over 300 years. Buying direct from the weavers supports the tradition and meets the people keeping it alive.

3. Red Piano Too Art Gallery & Local Culture

Frogmore on St. Helena Island is home to Red Piano Too, a celebrated gallery of local and national African American art. It's a hub for meeting artists, hearing stories, and understanding the visual side of Gullah Geechee expression.

4. Historic Churches and Praise Houses

Island congregations — from Brick Baptist Church (built in 1855) to tiny praise houses — preserve the call-and-response worship, spirituals, and ring-shout traditions that are the oldest surviving African American performance culture in America.

5. The Gullah Geechee Cultural Heritage Corridor

St. Helena sits at the center of a federally recognized corridor stretching from North Carolina to Florida. The island's land, language, food, and faith remain living testaments to a culture that has survived and thrived through extraordinary hardship.
