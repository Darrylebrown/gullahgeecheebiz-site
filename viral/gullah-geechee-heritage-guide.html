What is Gullah Geechee Culture?

The Gullah Geechee people are descendants of enslaved Africans who settled on the Sea Islands of South Carolina and Georgia. For over 400 years, they have preserved their unique language, traditions, food, and spirituality.

🌊 Language

The Gullah language blends English with West African grammar and vocabulary. Only a few thousand fluent speakers remain, making it one of the most endangered Creole languages in America.

🎨 Traditions

Sweetgrass basket weaving, ring shouts, praise house spirituality, and storytelling traditions connect the Gullah Geechee to their West African roots.

🍲 Food

Red rice, shrimp and grits, okra soup, benne wafers — Gullah Geechee cuisine is the foundation of Southern food and directly influenced American cooking.

📚 History

From the Combahee River Raid to Robert Smalls' heroic escape, Gullah Geechee people have shaped American history in profound ways.
