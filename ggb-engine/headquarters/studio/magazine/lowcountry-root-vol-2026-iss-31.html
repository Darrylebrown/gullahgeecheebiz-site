Cover Story

The Root of It All: Gullah Geechee Culture in July 2026

The feature article of the week — deep dive into Gullah Geechee culture, history, or community.

📷 Cover Story

The Gullah Geechee people have called the Sea Islands home for generations. Their culture — a living bridge to West Africa — is one of the oldest continuously preserved African American traditions in the United States.

From the language spoken in the praise houses to the sweetgrass baskets woven on St. Helena Island, every tradition tells a story of resilience, creativity, and deep connection to the land and water.

Today, as more people discover the beauty of the Lowcountry, the Gullah Geechee community continues to share its culture with the world — on its own terms.

In this issue of The Lowcountry Root, we explore the places, people, and traditions that make Gullah Geechee culture one of America's most treasured living legacies.

Sea Islands Journal

St. Helena: The Heart of the Sea Islands

Life on the Sea Islands — St. Helena, Hilton Head, Sapelo, Daufuskie, and beyond.

📷 Sea Islands Journal

St. Helena Island, South Carolina — home to Penn Center, one of the first schools for formerly enslaved people in the United States — remains the cultural heart of the Gullah Geechee community.

The island's quiet roads are lined with live oaks draped in Spanish moss. Historic churches dot the landscape. The marshes glow gold at sunset. And the people carry forward traditions that have been passed down for more than ten generations.

Visitors come for the beauty. They stay for the stories.

Sweetgrass & Baskets

The Art of the Sweetgrass Basket

The art and tradition of sweetgrass basket weaving — one of the oldest African art forms in America.

📷 Sweetgrass & Baskets

Sweetgrass basket weaving is one of the oldest African art forms in America, brought to the Lowcountry by enslaved West Africans and preserved through generations of Gullah Geechee artisans.

The baskets are made from sweetgrass, bulrush, pine needles, and palmetto fronds — all gathered from the Lowcountry landscape. Each basket takes days or weeks to complete, coiled by hand in a tradition that traces back to the rice-growing regions of West Africa.

Today, sweetgrass baskets are treasured as works of art, collected by museums and sold at markets along Highway 17 between Charleston and Savannah.

The Gullah Kitchen

A Taste of Home: Lowcountry Cooking

Recipes, food stories, and the culinary traditions of the Lowcountry.

📷 The Gullah Kitchen

Gullah Geechee cooking is the original Lowcountry cuisine — a fusion of West African ingredients and techniques with the bounty of the Sea Islands.

Red rice, shrimp and grits, okra soup, hoppin' John, benne wafers, sweet potato pie — these are the dishes that tell the story of a people who turned simple ingredients into a celebrated culinary tradition.

Every recipe carries history. Every meal is a connection to the ancestors.

The Living Tongue

The Gullah Language: A Living Bridge to Africa

The Gullah language — its roots, its speakers, and its future.

📷 The Living Tongue

The Gullah language is a creole language spoken by the Gullah Geechee people of the Sea Islands. It combines English with words and grammatical structures from West African languages, including Mende, Yoruba, Twi, and others.

Once suppressed in schools and public life, the Gullah language is now recognized as a vital part of America's linguistic heritage. Efforts to preserve and teach the language are growing, with classes, recordings, and cultural programs across the Lowcountry.

To hear Gullah spoken is to hear history alive.

Roots & Rhythms

Spirituals, Work Songs, and Praise House Music

Music of the Gullah Geechee — spirituals, work songs, praise houses, and modern expressions.

📷 Roots & Rhythms

Music is the heartbeat of Gullah Geechee culture. From the spirituals sung in praise houses to the work songs that kept rhythm in the fields, music has always been a source of strength, expression, and community.

The Gullah Geechee musical tradition has influenced American music in profound ways — from gospel to blues to jazz to folk. The call-and-response patterns, the polyrhythms, the deep emotional resonance — all trace back to the Sea Islands.

Today, a new generation of Gullah Geechee musicians is carrying the tradition forward.

Community Spotlight

Keeping the Culture Alive

Profiles of Gullah Geechee people, places, and organizations making a difference.

📷 Community Spotlight

Across the Lowcountry, Gullah Geechee community organizations are working to preserve and promote the culture. From the Gullah Geechee Cultural Heritage Corridor to local historical societies, the work of preservation is ongoing.

This week, we spotlight the people and organizations making a difference — the elders teaching the language, the artisans weaving the baskets, the cooks passing down the recipes, and the young people learning the traditions.

Tide Lines

The Waters That Shape Us

The waterways, marshes, and coast that define Gullah Geechee life.

📷 Tide Lines

The Gullah Geechee people have always lived close to the water. The creeks, rivers, marshes, and ocean of the Sea Islands are not just a backdrop — they are central to the culture, the economy, and the way of life.

Fishing, crabbing, oystering, and boat-building are traditions passed down through generations. The tides mark the rhythm of daily life. The marsh is both pantry and sanctuary.

To understand Gullah Geechee culture, you must understand the water.

Ancestors' Walk

Walking with the Ancestors

History, heritage, and the stories that shaped the Gullah Geechee people.

📷 Ancestors' Walk

The history of the Gullah Geechee people is a story of survival, resistance, and triumph. From the Middle Passage to the plantations of the Sea Islands, from the Civil War to Reconstruction, from the Civil Rights Movement to today — the Gullah Geechee community has endured and thrived.

Places like Penn Center, Mitchelville, and the Old Slave Mart Museum preserve this history. But the most important preservation happens in the stories passed down from generation to generation.

Creative Spirit

The Creative Spirit of the Lowcountry

Gullah Geechee art, craft, literature, and creative expression.

📷 Creative Spirit

Gullah Geechee art is as diverse as the culture itself — from sweetgrass baskets and quilts to painting, sculpture, literature, and film. The creative spirit runs deep in the Sea Islands.

Today, Gullah Geechee artists are gaining national recognition, their work collected by museums and celebrated at festivals. But the heart of the art remains the same: telling the story of a people, their land, and their culture.
