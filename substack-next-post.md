# The Gullah Geechee Corridor Is Having a Moment

## And you're part of it.

*Jul 25, 2026*

---

Three weeks ago, I posted a video of an alligator in a Lowcountry marsh.

I didn't think much of it. Just another day in the Gullah Geechee Corridor — the alligators are as much a part of the landscape as the marsh grass and the live oaks.

But the internet had other ideas.

That video has now been seen over **half a million times**. Half a million people watched an alligator float through a tidal creek and wanted to know more. They wanted to know about the place. The people. The culture that's been here for 300 years.

And that's what this newsletter is about.

---

### What's Happening

The Gullah Geechee Corridor is having a moment. Not because of one alligator video — but because people are hungry for real stories. Real culture. Real history that they didn't learn in school.

In the past month:

- **305 of you** have subscribed to The Root (welcome to the new folks — we're glad you're here)
- Our Pinterest pins have generated **1,740+ impressions** in their first week
- The **50-volume encyclopedia set** is being prepared for publication in 8 languages
- **4 audiobooks** are recorded and ready — English and Spanish
- A **full documentary** on heirs' property has been produced
- **186 branded logos** now represent Gullah Geechee culture across 30+ themes

This isn't a side project anymore. It's a movement.

### Why This Matters

The Gullah Geechee Corridor is one of the most culturally significant regions in America — and one of the most endangered. Development, climate change, and heirs' property loss are threatening a way of life that has survived for three centuries.

But here's the thing: the same internet that made an alligator video go viral can also help preserve this culture. Every share, every follow, every subscription — it all adds up.
