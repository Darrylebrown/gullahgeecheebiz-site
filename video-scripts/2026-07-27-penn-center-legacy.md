# Video Script — Documentary
## "The School That Freedom Built: Penn Center and the Gullah Geechee Legacy"

**Duration:** 12-15 minutes
**Format:** Documentary / YouTube
**Tone:** Reverent, cinematic, historically grounded, emotionally resonant
**Narrator:** Darryl Elliott Brown (or Gullah Geechee voice)

---

## SCENE 1 — COLD OPEN (0:00-1:00)

**Visual:** Drone shot at golden hour — St. Helena Island from above. Marsh grass swaying. Creeks snaking through the landscape. The drone descends toward a cluster of historic white buildings surrounded by live oaks draped in Spanish moss.

**Text overlay:** "St. Helena Island, South Carolina"

**Audio:** Wind through marsh grass. Distant birds. A single note from a cello, sustained.

**Narrator (VO):** "There are places in America where history is not just remembered. It is *felt*. This is one of those places."

**Visual:** Cut to a close-up of an old brick wall. Sunlight filtering through oak leaves. The camera pulls back to reveal the full facade of a building.

**Narrator (VO):** "Penn Center. The first school in the South for formerly enslaved people. A place where freedom was not just declared — it was *built*."

**Visual:** Title card fades in over the image:

**"THE SCHOOL THAT FREEDOM BUILT"**
**"Penn Center and the Gullah Geechee Legacy"**

---

## SCENE 2 — THE PORT ROYAL EXPERIMENT (1:00-2:30)

**Visual:** Archival photographs — black and white. Union soldiers arriving by ship. Enslaved people fleeing plantations. The Sea Islands in 1861.

**Audio:** Period-appropriate folk guitar. Soft, contemplative.

**Narrator (VO):** "In November 1861, the Union Navy captured Port Royal Sound. The white plantation owners fled inland, leaving behind 10,000 enslaved Gullah Geechee people on the Sea Islands. For the first time in their lives, they were free."

**Visual:** Map animation showing the Sea Islands — St. Helena, Hilton Head, Edisto, Beaufort. The Gullah Geechee Corridor highlighted.

**Narrator (VO):** "What happened next became known as the Port Royal Experiment — a radical test of whether formerly enslaved people could govern themselves, work their own land, and educate their own children."

**Visual:** Photograph of Laura Towne, a young white woman in 19th-century dress.

**Narrator (VO):** "In 1862, a 37-year-old abolitionist from Pennsylvania named Laura Towne arrived on St. Helena Island. She opened a school in a small cabin. She called it Penn School — named after the Pennsylvania abolitionist William Penn."

**Visual:** The cabin — small, wooden, humble. Then dissolve to the brick building that replaced it.

**Narrator (VO):** "That cabin became a brick building. That school became a movement. And that movement became the cornerstone of Gullah Geechee education for the next century."

---

## SCENE 3 — THE GULLAH GEECHEE CLASSROOM (2:30-4:00)

**Visual:** Reenactment or archival photos of Gullah Geechee children in a classroom circa 1900. Desks. Slates. A teacher at the front.

**Audio:** Children's voices reciting lessons. Soft piano.

**Narrator (VO):** "At Penn School, Gullah Geechee children learned to read and write in English — but they never lost their mother tongue. The Gullah language, with its West African grammar and vocabulary, was spoken in the yards, in the praise houses, and at the dinner table."

**Visual:** Close-up of hands weaving a sweetgrass basket. Then hands planting rice. Then hands playing a banjo.

**Narrator (VO):** "Penn School taught more than reading and arithmetic. It taught industrial skills — farming, carpentry, basket weaving, cooking. Skills that would allow Gullah Geechee families to build independent lives on the islands they had called home for generations."

**Visual:** Photograph of students in a carpentry workshop. Young Black men with saws and planes.

**Narrator (VO):** "By the 1920s, Penn School had become Penn Center — a community institution that served not just as a school, but as a hospital, a church, a community center, and a beacon of hope."

---

## SCENE 4 — THE CIVIL RIGHTS ERA (4:00-6:00)

**Visual:** Black and white footage — 1960s. Civil rights marches. Sit-ins. Then cut to Penn Center's campus — peaceful, quiet, surrounded by trees.

**Audio:** The music shifts — a low, tense undercurrent. The sound of footsteps on wooden floors.

**Narrator (VO):** "When the Civil Rights Movement swept the South, Penn Center became something new: a safe haven."

**Visual:** Photograph of Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. walking on Penn Center's campus. He is smiling, relaxed.

**Narrator (VO):** "Dr. Martin Luther King Jr. came to Penn Center multiple times between 1964 and 1967. He and the Southern Christian Leadership Conference used the campus as a retreat — a place to plan strategy, to rest, and to find the strength to continue."

**Visual:** Interior of a simple room. A bed. A desk. A window looking out at the oaks.

**Narrator (VO):** "In this room, Dr. King drafted portions of his speeches. In these halls, he walked and talked with other civil rights leaders. On these grounds, he found the quiet he needed to carry the weight of a movement."

**Visual:** Archival footage of Dr. King speaking. Then dissolve to Penn Center today — the same buildings, the same oaks.

**Narrator (VO):** "Penn Center was one of the only places in the South where Black leaders could gather without fear. It was, in every sense, sacred ground."

---

## SCENE 5 — THE GULLAH GEECHEE CULTURAL HERITAGE CORRIDOR (6:00-8:00)

**Visual:** A map of the Eastern Seaboard — from North Carolina to Florida. The Gullah Geechee Cultural Heritage Corridor highlighted in gold.

**Audio:** Uplifting, orchestral. A sense of arrival.

**Narrator (VO):** "In 2006, the U.S. Congress designated the Gullah Geechee Cultural Heritage Corridor — a federal recognition of the Gullah Geechee people's unique and irreplaceable contribution to American culture."

**Visual:** Montage — fast cuts of Gullah Geechee culture:
- Sweetgrass baskets on display
- A Gullah chef plating red rice
- A ring shout performance
- A Gullah storyteller with children gathered around
- Queen Quet addressing the United Nations

**Narrator (VO):** "Penn Center was named the official headquarters of the Corridor. The school that freedom built had become the keeper of an entire culture's story."

**Visual:** Interview clip — Emory Campbell (former Penn Center director), speaking directly to camera.

**Emory Campbell (on camera):** "Penn Center is not just a museum. It is a living institution. We are still educating. We are still preserving. We are still fighting for the land and the culture."

---

## SCENE 6 — FAMLEE DAY 2026 (8:00-10:00)

**Visual:** Footage from Gullah/Geechee Famlee Day — July 25, 2026. Crowds on Penn Center's lawn. Families picnicking. Children running. A stage with musicians.

**Audio:** Live recording of a Gullah spiritual choir. Hand claps. Feet stomping.

**Narrator (VO):** "Just two days ago, on July 25, 2026, Penn Center hosted Gullah/Geechee Famlee Day — the centerpiece of Gullah/Geechee Nation Appreciation Week."

**Visual:** Close-ups — a woman selling benne wafers. A blacksmith demonstrating ironwork. A sweetgrass weaver teaching a child the first coil.

**Narrator (VO):** "Thousands of people gathered on this campus — Gullah Geechee families from across the Corridor, visitors from around the world, and a new generation of young people learning what it means to carry this culture forward."

**Visual:** Queen Quet on stage, addressing the crowd.

**Queen Quet (on camera):** "Hunnuh mus tek cyare de root fa heal de tree. You must take care of the root to heal the tree. We are the root. And we are healing."

**Narrator (VO):** "The theme of this year's Appreciation Week — 'Take Care of the Root to Heal the Tree' — is a reminder that the work of preservation is never finished."

---

## SCENE 7 — THE THREATS (10:00-11:30)

**Visual:** Aerial shot of a new development — luxury condos rising next to a historic Gullah Geechee neighborhood. The contrast is jarring.

**Audio:** The music becomes somber. Minor key.

**Narrator (VO):** "But the root is under threat. Gentrification is pushing Gullah Geechee families off the islands their ancestors have lived on since the 1700s. Heirs' property — land passed down without a will — is being lost to developers at an alarming rate."

**Visual:** A Gullah Geechee elder standing on her front porch. Behind her, a "For Sale" sign on the neighboring property.

**Narrator (VO):** "Since the 1950s, Gullah Geechee land ownership on Hilton Head Island alone has dropped from over 80% to less than 10%. The story is similar across the Sea Islands."

**Visual:** Climate change imagery — flooded roads, eroded marshland, a storm approaching.

**Narrator (VO):** "And climate change threatens the very geography of the culture. Rising sea levels, stronger hurricanes, and saltwater intrusion are reshaping the Lowcountry. The islands that preserved Gullah Geechee culture for centuries are now fighting for their survival."

---

## SCENE 8 — THE RESILIENCE (11:30-13:00)

**Visual:** Montage of modern Gullah Geechee resilience:
- A community land trust meeting
- Young Gullah Geechee professionals in an office
- A Gullah language class at a university
- A Gullah chef being interviewed on a national cooking show

**Audio:** Building, hopeful. A single voice begins singing "Kumbaya" in Gullah.

**Narrator (VO):** "But the Gullah Geechee people have survived the Middle Passage. They have survived slavery. They have survived Reconstruction, Jim Crow, and the Civil Rights Movement. They will survive this."

**Visual:** Close-up of a young Gullah Geechee woman — maybe 22 years old — holding a sweetgrass basket she just finished weaving.

**Narrator (VO):** "A new generation is stepping up. They are learning the language. They are fighting for the land. They are building businesses, writing books, making films, and telling their own stories."

**Visual:** The young woman looks up and smiles.

**Young woman (on camera):** "I am Gullah Geechee. And I am not going anywhere."

---

## SCENE 9 — CONCLUSION (13:00-14:30)

**Visual:** Drone shot pulling back from Penn Center. The buildings grow smaller. The oaks spread their branches. The marsh stretches to the horizon.

**Audio:** The Gullah spiritual swells. Full choir. Triumphant.

**Narrator (VO):** "Penn Center stands as a monument to what freedom looks like when it is built by the people who fought for it. It is a school. It is a sanctuary. It is a symbol."

**Visual:** The drone rises higher. The island becomes a green jewel in a blue sea.

**Narrator (VO):** "And the Gullah Geechee people — the root of this tree — are still here. Still teaching. Still weaving. Still singing. Still praying. Still free."

**Visual:** Fade to black. Gold text on navy background:

**"Gullah Geechee Biz"**
**"Preserving a Culture. Telling a Story."**

**Text overlay:** "Penn Center | St. Helena Island, SC | penncenter.com"

**Audio:** The spiritual fades to a single voice, humming. Then silence.
