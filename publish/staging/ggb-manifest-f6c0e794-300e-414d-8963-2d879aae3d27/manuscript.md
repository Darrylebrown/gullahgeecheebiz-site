## Chapter 13.1: The Caldwell et al. (2025) Study

This volume provides the complete technical analysis of the most recent mtDNA study. Key findings:

### Sample Composition
- **79** self-identified Gullah Geechee participants
- **110** African American controls from AFDIL (Armed Forces DNA Identification Laboratory)

### Haplogroup Distribution
| Haplogroup | Gullah (n=79) | African Americans (n=110) |
|---|---|---|
| L0 | 2.5% | 3.1% |
| L1 | 8.9% | 12.4% |
| L2 | 28.0% | 34.6% |
| L3 | 54.7% | 38.8% |
| Non-L (European/Native Am.) | **0%** | ~5% |

### Statistical Analysis
- **Pearson's Chi-Square**: χ² = 115.62, df = 49, p = 2.63×10⁻⁷
- While significant association exists in overall structure, "no statistically significant difference between groups" in shared maternal ancestry patterns, supporting the progenitor hypothesis.

## Chapter 13.2: Sub-Haplogroup Analysis

### Most Frequent Sub-Haplogroups in Gullah
- **L2c**: 8/79 (10.1%)
- **L3e2b**: 6/79 (7.6%)
- **L3f1**: 5/79 (6.3%)

### Key Differential Finding
**L2a1**, most frequent in African Americans (16/110, 14.5%), was found in only 2/79 (2.5%) Gullah participants, suggesting differential representation of specific West African populations.

## Chapter 13.3: The L3 Haplogroup & Bantu Expansion

L3 is "an important marker for Bantu-speaking populations, originated 70,000 years ago during the Out-of-Africa expansion." Its high frequency in Gullah (54.7%) suggests significant representation of Bantu-speaking ancestors from Central and West Africa.

---

## Chapter 13.4: The Progenitor Hypothesis

Caldwell et al. (2025) hypothesize that "Legacy African Americans genetically, and more so maternally descend from the Sea Islands and coastal Low country that insulated the Gullah," positioning the Gullah Geechee as a "founding biocultural population for Legacy African Americans."
