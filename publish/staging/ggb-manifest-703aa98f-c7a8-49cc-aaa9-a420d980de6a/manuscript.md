## Chapter 18.1: The Voyage of the Hare

The slave ship *Hare* arrived in Charleston in 1756 carrying captives from Sierra Leone, including a 10-year-old girl named Priscilla. This volume reconstructs the voyage from original documents.

### The Paper Trail
"The extraordinary paper trail begins with a letter from a slave ship captain dated April 8, 1756. Records show that the ship purchased some of its slaves at Bunce Island, and thus Priscilla may have passed through there."

### The Captain's Letter
The captain's correspondence, preserved in the South Carolina Historical Society, documents the procurement of captives along the Sierra Leone coast, including transactions at Bunce Island and other trading posts.

## Chapter 18.2: Priscilla's Descendants

Edward Ball, researching his own family's slaveholding history, discovered that "one of his ancestors purchased Priscilla, and through family records he was able to link the little girl to her modern descendants." The Martin Family of Charleston are Priscilla's 7th-generation descendants.

### The Lineage
- Priscilla (c. 1746-?) — Arrived Charleston 1756, age 10
- [Subsequent generations documented through plantation records, census data, and family Bibles]
- Thomalind Martin Polite (b. 1973) — 7th-generation descendant

## Chapter 18.3: Priscilla's Homecoming (2005)

In May 2005, Thomalind Polite arrived in Sierra Leone for a week of national celebration. The journey included:

1. **Freetown**: Reception by government officials and cultural leaders
2. **Bunce Island**: Walking through the slave yard where Priscilla likely waited
3. **Mende Country**: Visiting regions where Priscilla may have originated
4. **The Return**: "When Mrs. Polite entered the slave yard for women and children, she said she could feel Priscilla's presence by 'walking where she walked.'"
