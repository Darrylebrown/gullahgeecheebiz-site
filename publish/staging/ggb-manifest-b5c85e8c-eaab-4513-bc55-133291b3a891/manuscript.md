## Chapter 14.1: The Zhang et al. (2021) Study

This landmark study analyzed **883** unrelated Gullah African Americans using whole-genome SNP arrays, compared with **1,322** African Americans from the Jackson Heart Study (JHS), **381** Sierra Leone Africans, and reference panels from Africa and Europe.

---

## Chapter 14.2: Ancestry Proportions

### Global Ancestry Estimates (qpAdm)

| Population | Autosomal African | X-Chromosome African | European Admixture | Native American |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gullah (n=883) | 90.7% [90.6-90.8] | 93.4% [92.9-93.9] | ~7.5% | ~1.8% |
| JHS AA (n=1322) | 82.2% [82.1-82.3] | 86.5% [86.0-87.0] | ~16.5% | ~1.3% |

The Gullah show significantly higher African ancestry proportions across all metrics.

---

## Chapter 14.3: Male-Biased European Admixture

The study found "increased male-biased European admixture" in the Gullah, with an estimated **45.53%** of ancestry coming from males (vs. 42.15% in JHS African Americans). This reflects the well-documented sexual exploitation of enslaved African women by European slave owners.

---

## Chapter 14.4: Population Bottleneck

"A slightly tighter bottleneck in the Gullah 13 generations ago suggests a largely shared demographic history with non-Gullah African Americans" but with evidence of founder effects consistent with isolation on the Sea Islands.

---

## Chapter 14.5: Sierra Leone Affinity

"Despite a slightly higher relatedness to populations from Sierra Leone, our data demonstrate that the Gullah are genetically related to many West African populations." The study confirms that "Sierra Leone is not the sole origin point" and that the Gullah "share a common ancestry with numerous populations from Sierra Leone and other regions of West Africa."
