# The Gullah Geechee Sea Island Coalition  

## Introduction  

The Gullah Geechee Sea Island Coalition (GGSIC) is a pivotal organization dedicated to preserving and promoting the cultural heritage of the Gullah Geechee people, descendants of enslaved Africans who have inhabited the Sea Islands of the southeastern United States for centuries. Established in 1996 by Queen Quet Marquetta Goodwine, the Chieftess of the Gullah Geechee Nation, the Coalition emerged in response to the urgent need to safeguard Gullah Geechee traditions, land, and identity amidst rapid coastal development and cultural erasure. Rooted in the principle of self-determination, the GGSIC works to empower Gullah Geechee communities through education, advocacy, and cultural preservation initiatives.  

The Gullah Geechee culture, a unique blend of African, European, and Indigenous influences, is deeply tied to the land and waterways of the Sea Islands, stretching from North Carolina to Florida. The Coalition’s work is not only a fight for cultural survival but also a testament to the resilience and creativity of a people who have maintained their distinct language, cuisine, crafts, and spiritual practices despite centuries of oppression. This chapter explores the history, mission, and impact of the Gullah Geechee Sea Island Coalition, highlighting its role as a guardian of Gullah Geechee heritage.  

## The Founding of the Gullah Geechee Sea Island Coalition  

The Gullah Geechee Sea Island Coalition was founded in 1996 by Queen Quet Marquetta Goodwine, a visionary leader and cultural activist. Born and raised on St. Helena Island, South Carolina, Queen Quet witnessed firsthand the encroachment of developers and the erosion of Gullah Geechee traditions. Her commitment to preserving her people’s heritage led her to establish the Coalition as a grassroots organization focused on advocacy, education, and cultural preservation.  

The Coalition’s founding was driven by several key events, including the displacement of Gullah Geechee communities due to resort developments and the construction of highways that disrupted traditional ways of life. Queen Quet’s leadership brought national attention to these issues, culminating in her historic testimony before the United States Congress in 2006. Her efforts played a crucial role in the establishment of the Gullah Geechee Cultural Heritage Corridor, a National Heritage Area designated by Congress in 2006 to protect and celebrate Gullah Geechee culture.  

## Mission and Objectives  

The GGSIC’s mission is multifaceted, encompassing cultural preservation, land retention, and community empowerment. At its core, the Coalition seeks to ensure that the Gullah Geechee people remain stewards of their ancestral lands and traditions. Key objectives include:  

1. **Cultural Preservation**: Documenting and promoting Gullah Geechee language, arts, crafts, music, and spiritual practices.  
2. **Land Retention**: Protecting Gullah Geechee land from exploitation and ensuring its rightful ownership by descendants.  
3. **Education and Advocacy**: Raising awareness about Gullah Geechee history and advocating for policies that support cultural and environmental sustainability.  
4. **Economic Development**: Supporting Gullah Geechee entrepreneurship and sustainable tourism initiatives.  

The Coalition’s work is guided by the principle of Sankofa, a West African Adinkra symbol meaning “go back and fetch it,” emphasizing the importance of learning from the past to build a better future.  

## Cultural Preservation Initiatives  

One of the Coalition’s most significant achievements is its role in preserving the Gullah Geechee language, often referred to as Gullah or Geechee. This creole language, rooted in West and Central African languages and English, is a cornerstone of Gullah Geechee identity. The Coalition has supported linguistic research, oral history projects, and educational programs to ensure the language’s survival.  

The organization also promotes traditional arts and crafts, such as sweetgrass basket weaving, a skill passed down through generations. Through workshops and exhibitions, the Coalition has helped revitalize this ancient art form, providing economic opportunities for artisans while preserving cultural heritage.  

Music and spiritual practices are other focal points of the Coalition’s preservation efforts. Gullah Geechee spiritual traditions, including ring shouts and praise houses, are celebrated through community events and educational programs. The Coalition has also partnered with scholars and musicians to archive and promote Gullah Geechee music, from traditional spirituals to contemporary compositions.  

## Advocacy and Policy Work  

The GGSIC has been at the forefront of advocacy efforts to protect Gullah Geechee land and culture. One of its most notable achievements was the establishment of the Gullah Geechee Cultural Heritage Corridor, a 12,000-square-mile area spanning four states. This designation provides federal support for cultural preservation and sustainable development projects.  

The Coalition has also been instrumental in raising awareness about environmental threats to the Sea Islands, such as sea-level rise and industrial pollution. Through partnerships with environmental organizations, the GGSIC advocates for policies that protect the fragile ecosystems of the region while respecting Gullah Geechee land rights.  

In addition to its policy work, the Coalition has supported legal efforts to reclaim land lost due to fraudulent practices like heirs’ property laws. By providing educational resources and legal assistance, the GGSIC empowers Gullah Geechee families to retain ownership of ancestral lands.  

## Education and Community Outreach  

Education is a central pillar of the GGSIC’s mission. The organization has developed a range of educational programs, including school curricula, workshops, and cultural festivals. These initiatives aim to teach both Gullah Geechee youth and the broader public about the rich history and contributions of the Gullah Geechee people.  

The Coalition’s annual Gullah Geechee Cultural Heritage Festival, held on St. Helena Island, is a highlight of its outreach efforts. The festival features performances, demonstrations, and lectures that celebrate Gullah Geechee culture. Through these events, the Coalition fosters a sense of pride and continuity within the community while sharing its heritage with the world.  

## Economic Development and Sustainability  

The GGSIC recognizes the importance of economic sustainability in preserving Gullah Geechee culture. The organization supports Gullah Geechee entrepreneurs through microloans, business training, and marketing assistance. By promoting local artisans, farmers, and tourism operators, the Coalition helps create economic opportunities that align with cultural values.  

Sustainable tourism is another focus of the Coalition’s economic development efforts. Through initiatives like the Gullah Geechee Cultural Heritage Trail, the organization encourages visitors to explore the Sea Islands in a way that respects and supports local communities.  

## Conclusion  

The Gullah Geechee Sea Island Coalition stands as a beacon of resilience and cultural pride. Through its tireless efforts, the organization has protected and promoted the unique heritage of the Gullah Geechee people, ensuring that their traditions and land endure for future generations. Queen Quet’s visionary leadership and the unwavering commitment of the GGSIC exemplify the power of grassroots activism in preserving cultural identity.  

As the Gullah Geechee community faces new challenges, from climate change to gentrification, the Coalition’s work remains as vital as ever. Its emphasis on self-determination, education, and collaboration serves as a model for cultural preservation efforts worldwide. The Gullah Geechee Sea Island Coalition is not only safeguarding a legacy but also creating a path for a sustainable and inclusive future.
