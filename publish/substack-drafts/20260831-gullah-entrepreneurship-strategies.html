How to build sustainable enterprises rooted in Gullah Geechee culture — without selling your soul

I get asked this question a lot: How do you build a business when your culture has been exploited for centuries?

It's a fair question. I've seen what happens when Gullah Geechee culture gets packaged, commodified, and sold back to us at a premium. The sweetgrass baskets made by Gullah women become designer home decor. The recipes become trendy soul food without the history. The language becomes a novelty.

So how do you build something authentic?

Here's what I've learned building Gullah Geechee Biz:

**Anchor in truth, not trend.** Don't chase what's popular. Build on what's true. Our culture has 300+ years of documented history. That's not a trend — that's heritage. Let that be your foundation.

**Tell the whole story.** When you share Gullah Geechee culture in business, don't give people the highlight reel. Give them the full narrative — the struggle, the resilience, the complexity. That's what makes it authentic.

**Compensate the community.** If you're profiting from Gullah Geechee culture, some of that profit should stay in the community. This isn't charity — it's justice. It's also good business. Communities that see returns invest back.

**Build for longevity, not virality.** That alligator video got 500K views. But it won't pay the bills. Sustainable businesses are built on relationships, not reach. Focus on deep connections with fewer people.

**Protect your intellectual property.** Gullah Geechee knowledge is not public domain. Your research, your formulations, your curation — these are valuable. Patent what you can. Copyright what you should. Be deliberate about what you share freely.

**Create multiple revenue streams.** One product, one platform, one income source is vulnerability. Diversify. Books, courses, memberships, merchandise, speaking — build a portfolio.

**Partner with cultural advisors.** Before you launch anything Gullah-related, get feedback from Gullah community members. Not as a checkbox — as genuine partnership. Their perspective will save you from mistakes you didn't know you could make.

**Measure success beyond money.** Yes, revenue matters. But so does cultural impact. How many people learned something new? How many felt proud? How many shared your work with someone who needed to hear it?

The businesses that will thrive in the Gullah Geechee Corridor aren't the ones that extract value — they're the ones that create it. That honor the culture while building sustainable enterprises.

That's the work. That's the mission.
