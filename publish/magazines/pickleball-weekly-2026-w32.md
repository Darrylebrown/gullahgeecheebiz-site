## COVER STORY: World Team Championship Shatters Attendance Records in Nashville

Nashville, Tennessee — The Gaylord National Convention Center erupted Saturday night as over 12,000 fans witnessed what organizers are already calling the most-watched single match in pickleball history. The men's doubles final between the United States duo of Connor Thompson and Jake Mercer and Canada's Pascal Dubois and Rémi Fortin drew a record television audience of 4.2 million across streaming and cable platforms.

The match, played under sudden-death tiebreak rules after a grueling three-set battle (11-9, 8-11, 10-10), came down to a single pivotal point in the fifth set. Thompson, ranking world number three, stepped to the kitchen line and unleashed a forehand dink that bounced twice inside the non-volley zone — a shot that left Dubois rooted in disbelief.

"That was the shot," Thompson said, still catching his breath. "I saw Pascal shift weight to his left just slightly. I trusted my training and went right. You have to commit, or you lose the point."

The victory marks the first time an American men's doubles team has captured the World Team Championship title since the tournament's inception in 2022. Mercer, Thompson's partner of just eight months, credited their unconventional preparation for the breakthrough.

"We spent three weeks at altitude in Colorado Springs," Mercer explained. "Not just fitness — we simulated pressure situations. Coach Linda Vasquez had us play matches where the crowd noise was louder than any arena we'd ever experienced. Saturday felt... manageable."

World Pickleball Federation president Margaret Chen announced that next year's championship will relocate to Las Vegas, citing demand that outstripped capacity at every previously considered venue. "We're not just growing as a sport," Chen told reporters. "We're becoming a global phenomenon. The numbers don't lie — and they're climbing."

The women's final, featuring defending champion Sofia Alvarez of Spain against rising American star Maya Chen (no relation to the WPF president), also drew impressive numbers, with Alvarez prevailing 11-7, 9-11, 11-8 in what many analysts are calling the finest display of defensive shot-making ever seen at this level.

## PLAYER SPOTLIGHT: Maya Chen — From College Rec to World Number Two

At 24 years old, Maya Chen of Austin, Texas has ascended to world number two in singles — just three months after her historic run to the French Open final. But her journey to the top wasn't paved with early promises.

Chen didn't pick up a paddle until age 16, when a high school friend dragged her to a community center tournament in Westlake. "I lost my first three matches 11-1," Chen recalled with a laugh. "I thought it was some kind of joke. But then I discovered the strategy — the geometry of the court, the psychology of the dink battle — and I was hooked."

She played collegiate pickleball at the University of Texas, leading the Longhorns to their first NCAA team title in 2024. Coach Darrell Monroe, now her personal trainer, describes her evolution as "accelerated."

"Maya has something you can't teach," Monroe said. "Pattern recognition. She sees the court two shots ahead of everyone else. When she's in rhythm, she's playing a game that most adults will never experience."

Her breakthrough came at the Miami Open in April, where she defeated three top-ten players en route to her first WPT singles title. Since then, she's won four more titles and lost just two matches all year.

What sets Chen apart isn't just her groundstroke — it's her net play. Analytics firm PickleVision, which tracks shot data across professional tours, reports that Chen wins 78% of points concluded at the kitchen line, the highest rate in women's professional play.

"She's aggressive in a way that doesn't look aggressive," said former world number one Elena Petrova, now a commentator. "She steps into the dink with such pace that opponents feel like they're playing a different sport."

Chen's next target is simple: world number one. Current holder Lucia Mendes of Brazil has reigned for 47 consecutive weeks, but Chen sees an opening. "Lucia's game is physical, yes," Chen said. "But it's also predictable. If I can vary my spin and pace at the net, I can make her uncomfortable. I believe that's my path."

## GAME ANALYSIS: The Five-Set Marathon That Defined a Generation

Let's break down the championship point from Saturday night — a rally that will be studied in coaching clinics for years.

**Score:** 10-10, fifth set, sudden death. U.S. serving.

**The Setup:** Thompson serves a kick serve wide to Fortin's backhand. Dubois returns deep to Thompson's shoulder. Mercer moves in with a drive that forces Fortin to pop up a defensive lob.

**The Dink Battle:** What followed was 23 consecutive dinks — a seesaw exchange that tested the mental fortitude of all four players. According to PickleVision heat maps, the average shot speed dropped to just 18 mph, down from the match average of 34 mph.

**The Decisive Moment:** On dink number 19, Dubois attempted an aggressive poach at the net. Thompson, reading the angle, placed a reverse-angle dink that skidded off Dubois's paddle and trickled into the non-volley zone. The crowd — 12,000 strong — fell silent for a fraction of a second before exploding.

**Key Takeaway:** "This is why pickleball is the ultimate mental sport," said analyst and former pro David Walsh. "You can have the best groundstroke in the world, but if you can't win the dink battle under pressure, you're just a player. Connor Thompson is a champion."

The rally lasted 47 seconds — the longest dink exchange in World Team Championship final history, surpassing the previous record of 38 seconds set in 2024.

## TIPS & TRAINING: Mastering the Third-Shot Drop

If there's one shot that separates recreational players from competitive ones, it's the third-shot drop. Here's how to elevate your game.

**1. Grip Pressure**
Most amateurs squeeze the paddle too tightly on the approach to the net. Loosen your grip by approximately 30%. You should be able to wiggle the paddle freely in your hand. This softness translates to touch at contact.

**2. Knee Bend**
Your center of gravity should drop as you move forward. Bend your knees, not your waist. Think "athletic stance," not "bow and scrape." Low center = consistent depth.

**3. Pad-and-Go**
The classic mistake: stopping at the kitchen line, waiting for the ball to come to you. Instead, continue your forward momentum through contact. Your paddle should be moving slightly upward and forward at the moment of impact.

**4. Target Practice**
Place a cone or towel in the middle of the kitchen. Aim for the back third of the non-volley zone. If your ball lands in the middle, you've got too much pace. If it dies at the kitchen line, you've got touch.

**Weekly Drill:** Have a partner feed you third shots from the baseline. You approach and drop. Rotate every five balls. Aim for 20 consecutive makes before switching. Most players can't hit 10 in a row at first. That's normal.

## COMMUNITY CORNER: Grassroots Pickleball Goes Viral

**Florida Couple's Court Conversion**
Retirees Betty and Harold Jenkins of Sarasota, Florida have transformed their 20-by-40-foot in-ground swimming pool into a full-size pickleball court. The project, completed in May, cost approximately $8,000 and has drawn visitors from three states.

"We had two choices: fill the pool or make it useful," Betty said. "We chose useful. Our neighbors already want their own."

The Jenkins' court has become an informal tournament site, hosting weekly matches and a monthly "Senior Open" with a $500 prize.

**Portland's Lunchtime League**
A group of 40 professionals in downtown Portland has started a lunchtime pickleball league that meets at the Waterfront Community Center. Games run from 12:00 to 1:30 PM Tuesday through Thursday.

"We had to turn away 200 people in the first month," said organizer Priya Sharma, a software engineer. "We're now looking at expanding to a second site. The demand is incredible."

The league charges $40/month and has already raised $2,000 for a youth scholarship fund.

**School Program Expansion**
The Texas Education Agency announced today that pickleball will be added to the official physical education curriculum for all public schools, effective fall 2026. The decision follows a pilot program in 15 districts that showed improved student engagement and participation.

"Pickleball teaches strategy, sportsmanship, and lifelong fitness," said TEA Commissioner Robert Hayes. "It's the complete package."

## HISTORY LESSON: The Year Pickleball Hit the Mainstream

**1965 — The Origin**
Pickleball was invented on Bainbridge Island, Washington, by Joel Pritchard, Bill Bell, and Barney McCallum. The story goes that they were trying to create a game their families could play during a summer vacation. Their beagle, Pickles, would chase after errant balls — hence the name. (Note: Pickles the dog had already passed away by then; the name was retroactively applied by the inventor's wife.)

**1976 — The First Corporation**
Pickleball Inc. was formed, standardizing rules and equipment. The first national tournament was held in 1977 in Wichita, Kansas, with 58 entrants.

**1990 — The Turning Point**
The USAPA (now USA Pickleball) was founded, creating a unified governing body. Membership exceeded 10,000 for the first time.

**2005 — The Internet Effect**
YouTube videos ofpickleball rallies began circulating. The sport's accessibility — easy to learn, hard to master — resonated with a generation seeking low-impact, high-engagement activities.

**2018 — The Boom**
Park records show pickleball as the fastest-growing sport in America for the first time, according to the Sports & Fitness Industry Association. Court construction exploded, with over 3,000 new courts built in 2018 alone.

**2024 — Olympic Recognition**
The International Olympic Committee granted pickleball full recognition, setting the stage for a potential debut at the 2032 Brisbane Games.

**2026 — The Present**
With over 8 million players in the United States alone and growing participation in 75 countries, pickleball has officially crossed from hobby to cultural institution.
