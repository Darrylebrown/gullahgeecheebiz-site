# TikTok Script — July 27, 2026
## "The Alligator and the Ancestors: Gullah/Geechee Nation Appreciation Week"

**Duration:** 60 seconds
**Hook:** Animal/Nature → Gullah Culture Pivot
**Audio:** Low, atmospheric marsh sounds → build to triumphant Gullah spiritual

---

### SCENE 1 (0:00-0:08) — THE HOOK
**Visual:** Close-up of an alligator's eye, half-submerged in blackwater. Slow zoom out to show the full reptile gliding through a Lowcountry creek. Spanish moss dripping from ancient oaks overhead.

**Text overlay:** "This creature has been here since the dinosaurs..."

**Audio:** Deep, wet bass notes. Water sounds. A single cricket chirp.

---

### SCENE 2 (0:08-0:15) — THE PIVOT
**Visual:** Cut to the same creek, but now we see the banks — an old Gullah Geechee man in a wide-brimmed hat, casting a net from a wooden dock. The alligator swims past him. He doesn't flinch.

**Text overlay:** "And the Gullah Geechee have been here just as long."

**Audio:** The bass fades. A distant praise house spiritual begins — "Wade in the Water."

---

### SCENE 3 (0:15-0:30) — THE STORY
**Visual:** Montage — quick cuts:
- Penn Center's historic brick buildings on St. Helena Island
- Sweetgrass baskets being coiled by weathered brown hands
- Queen Quet speaking at a podium, wearing traditional regalia
- A ring shout circle, feet moving in the dirt

**Text overlay:** "Gullah/Geechee Nation Appreciation Week 2026"

**Voiceover (warm, authoritative):** "This week, the Gullah Geechee Nation is celebrating Appreciation Week. Famlee Day just happened at Penn Center — the first school for freed people in the South. Over 160 years of resilience."

---

### SCENE 4 (0:30-0:45) — THE DEPTH
**Visual:** Slow pan across a praise house interior. Wooden pews. A single window letting in golden light. Then cut to an aerial drone shot of the Sea Islands — marsh, ocean, and land intertwined.

**Text overlay:** "Hunnuh mus tek cyare de root fa heal de tree"

**Voiceover:** "Queen Quet says it best: 'You must take care of the root to heal the tree.' The alligator knows its place in the marsh. The Gullah Geechee know their place in this land. And we're still here."

---

### SCENE 5 (0:45-0:60) — THE CALL TO ACTION
**Visual:** Return to the alligator — now fully emerged on a muddy bank, sun warming its back. Cut to a Gullah Geechee family on a porch, three generations, laughing. The alligator slips back into the water.

**Text overlay:** "Gullah Geechee Biz — Preserving a Culture. Telling a Story."

**Voiceover:** "This week, learn the language. Support the businesses. Protect the land. The root is strong. The tree is healing."

**Audio:** The spiritual swells to a triumphant finish. Fade to black.

**End card:** Navy background, gold text: "Gullah Geechee Biz" + "Follow for more culture."

---

### PRODUCTION NOTES
- **Location scouting:** Penn Center, St. Helena Island; any Lowcountry blackwater creek (ACE Basin recommended)
- **Alligator footage:** Use stock or pre-shot B-roll — do NOT approach wild alligators for this shoot
- **Talent:** Cast actual Gullah Geechee community members, not actors
- **Music clearance:** "Wade in the Water" is public domain; arrange with a Gullah spiritual choir
- **Hashtags:** #GullahGeechee #GullahGeecheeNation #PennCenter #Lowcountry #Alligator #CulturePreservation #GullahGeecheeBiz
