**Thread: 10 Things You Didn't Know About Gullah Geechee Culture 🧵**

1/ The Gullah Geechee people are recognized by UNESCO as having preserved West African culture more completely than any other diaspora community in the Americas. 🌍

2/ Their language, Gullah, is a creole with strong roots in West African languages. Some words trace back to Mende, Kikongo, and Igbo. 🗣️

3/ Sweetgrass basket weaving is a 300-year-old tradition. Each basket takes 2-3 weeks to make and was historically worth $1,000 in today's money. 🧺

4/ The Ring Shout is the oldest Black American dance form — it predates jazz, blues, and hip-hop. It's still performed in Sea Islands churches today. 💃

5/ Red rice, hoppin' john, and shrimp and grits all have direct West African origins. The Gullah people brought these recipes from Senegambia. 🍚

6/ Penn Center on St. Helena Island was one of the first schools for formerly enslaved people after the Civil War. It's now a National Historic Landmark. 🏫

7/ Indigo dye, used by Gullah artisans, was once worth more than gold. The blue cloth trade connected the Lowcountry to global markets. 💙

8/ The Gullah Geechee Cultural Heritage Corridor spans 125 miles of coastline from Wilmington, NC to Jacksonville, FL. It's the largest such corridor in the US. 🗺️

9/ Marsh tide clock — Gullah fishermen read the tides like a clock, knowing exactly when fish would be available based on lunar cycles. 🌊

10/ Today, there are an estimated 250,000-1 million Gullah Geechee people. Our Encyclopedia Collection preserves their history for future generations. 📚
