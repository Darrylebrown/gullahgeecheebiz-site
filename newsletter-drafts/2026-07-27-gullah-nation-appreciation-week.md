# Hunnuh Mus Tek Cyare de Root fa Heal de Tree: Gullah/Geechee Nation Appreciation Week 2026

**By Darryl Elliott Brown | Gullah Geechee Biz**

---

This week, the Gullah/Geechee Nation is celebrating something extraordinary. From Jacksonville, North Carolina, down to Jacksonville, Florida — across the Sea Islands, the marshlands, and the coastal communities that have held our culture for over three centuries — families, elders, artists, and advocates are gathering for **Gullah/Geechee Nation Appreciation Week 2026**.

And the theme this year, spoken by our Chieftess Queen Quet herself, cuts straight to the bone:

> *"Hunnuh mus tek cyare de root fa heal de tree."*

You must take care of the root to heal the tree.

---

## A Week That Matters

Gullah/Geechee Nation Appreciation Week was formally instituted in 2012, building on the historic declaration of sovereignty made on July 2, 2000, at Sullivan's Island, South Carolina — the very ground where so many of our ancestors first set foot in this land. Since then, municipalities from North Carolina to Florida have issued proclamations recognizing the week, honoring the living legacy of the Gullah Geechee people.

This year's celebration kicked off with **Gullah/Geechee Famlee Day** on July 24-25 at the Penn Center National Historic Landmark District on St. Helena Island — the very heart of Gullah Geechee culture. The CREATE Artists Collective Reception and Exhibition brought together visual artists, basket weavers, quilters, and storytellers whose work carries the memory of West Africa in every stitch, coil, and brushstroke.

## Why This Week Matters Right Now

The Gullah Geechee people are at a crossroads. Our ancestral lands face threats from rising sea levels, gentrification, and development pressure that has accelerated in the past decade. Heirs' property — land passed down through generations without formal wills — remains vulnerable to predatory developers. And yet, the culture endures.

What makes Gullah/Geechee Nation Appreciation Week so powerful is that it is not a passive celebration. It is an active declaration: *We are still here. We are still Gullah. We are still Geechee. And we are not going anywhere.*

The week highlights three pillars that define the modern Gullah Geechee movement:

**Sovereignty and Self-Determination.** The Gullah/Geechee Nation is recognized as a sovereign people, and this week honors the ongoing journey of human rights protection for Afro-indigenous descendants on the Sea Islands.

**Land and Environmental Protection.** Sea Island ecosystems face existential threats. The week amplifies efforts to keep ancestral lands in Gullah Geechee hands and protect the coastal environment that has sustained our people for generations.

**Cultural and Economic Empowerment.** From sweetgrass basket weavers on Charleston's Market Street to Gullah chefs redefining Lowcountry cuisine, the week encourages support for native Gullah Geechee businesses, artists, and historic landmarks.

## What's Coming Next

The celebration continues through early August. If you're in the Lowcountry, look for community gatherings, cultural demonstrations, and educational events across the Gullah Geechee Corridor. And if you can't be here in person, the Gullah/Geechee Sea Island Coalition hosts virtual programming that brings the culture to you.

## How You Can Honor the Week

Whether you're Gullah Geechee by blood or an ally who loves the culture, here are three ways to participate:

1. **Support Gullah Geechee businesses.** Buy a sweetgrass basket directly from a weaver. Order from a Gullah-owned restaurant. Purchase books by Gullah authors.

2. **Learn the language.** Gullah is the only African-American Creole language in the United States. Learn a phrase this week. "How oonuh dey?" (How are you?) is a good place to start.

3. **Protect the land.** Support organizations working to preserve heirs' property and combat gentrification in Gullah Geechee communities. The land is not just dirt — it is memory, it is identity, it is legacy.

---

As Queen Quet reminds us, the root must be cared for if the tree is to heal. This week, let us all — Gullah, Geechee, and friends alike — tend to the root.
